According to the [National Park](https://npfruskagora.co.rs/en/animals), roe deer and wild boar are still common, while the wildcat, badger, pine marten and the dormice are rarer or declining. The figures for the whole mountain (about 60 mammal species, 15 bat species, 13 amphibians and 11 reptiles) come from the [Wikipedia article on Fruška Gora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fru%C5%A1ka_Gora).

## Large mammals

{{< atlas >}}
{{< species icon="deer" latin="Capreolus capreolus" name="Roe deer" >}}Still present in good numbers, says the National Park. Look for it at dawn and dusk at the forest edge and in the meadows above the village.{{< /species >}}
{{< species icon="boar" latin="Sus scrofa" level="caution" name="Wild boar" >}}Common in the forest; in autumn it turns over the ground in search of acorns. Keep away from a sow with piglets.{{< /species >}}
{{< species icon="deer" latin="Cervus elaphus" name="Red deer" >}}Bred in the National Park's hunting reserves; the Vorovo reserve is closed to the public.{{< /species >}}
{{< species icon="deer" latin="Dama dama" name="Fallow deer" >}}Brought back to the mountain: a deer with white spots on a reddish coat and broad, shovel-shaped antlers.{{< /species >}}
{{< species icon="mouflon" latin="Ovis gmelini musimon" name="Mouflon" >}}A wild sheep with curled horns. In 2018 thirty animals were brought from Slovakia, raising the park's herd to about 70.{{< /species >}}
{{< species icon="bison" latin="Bison bonasus" level="caution" name="European bison" >}}Europe's largest land animal has lived in a fenced part of the National Park since 2022; in 2025 there were six. If you ever meet one outside the fence, keep your distance.{{< /species >}}
{{< species icon="jackal" latin="Canis aureus" name="Golden jackal" >}}A newcomer: the National Park records it as a species new to the area. At night its howling chorus can carry from the fields.{{< /species >}}
{{< /atlas >}}

## Small mammals and bats

{{< atlas >}}
{{< species icon="mustelid" latin="Meles meles" name="Badger" >}}A night-time digger with a black-and-white striped face. The National Park lists it among species that are rare or declining.{{< /species >}}
{{< species icon="jackal" latin="Felis silvestris" level="rare" name="Wildcat" >}}Shy and rare; it looks like a large tabby cat with a thick, blunt, ringed tail.{{< /species >}}
{{< species icon="mustelid" latin="Martes martes" name="Pine marten" >}}A climber of the treetops with a yellowish throat patch. According to the National Park its numbers have grown recently.{{< /species >}}
{{< species icon="dormouse" latin="Glis glis" name="Edible dormouse" >}}A grey, squirrel-like night animal that sleeps through the winter, often in hollow trees.{{< /species >}}
{{< species icon="dormouse" latin="Muscardinus avellanarius" name="Hazel dormouse" >}}A tiny golden-brown dormouse of hazel and bramble thickets.{{< /species >}}
{{< species icon="dormouse" latin="Spermophilus citellus" level="protected" name="European ground squirrel" >}}A protected natural rarity of open grassland; it lives in colonies and whistles when alarmed.{{< /species >}}
{{< species icon="bat" latin="Chiroptera" level="protected" name="Bats" >}}15 of Serbia's 30 bat species live on the mountain, and all of them are strictly protected. At dusk you can see them hunting insects.{{< /species >}}
{{< /atlas >}}

## Amphibians, reptiles and insects

{{< atlas >}}
{{< species icon="salamander" latin="Salamandra salamandra" level="caution" name="Fire salamander" >}}Black and yellow, out on forest paths after rain. Its skin releases a toxin: don't pick it up, and wash your hands if you did.{{< /species >}}
{{< species icon="snake" latin="Vipera berus" level="poison" name="Adder" >}}A venomous snake with a dark zigzag along its back. Bites are rare and rarely fatal, but if bitten, keep calm and get medical help at once (194).{{< /species >}}
{{< species icon="beetle" latin="Lucanus cervus" level="protected" name="Stag beetle" >}}Europe's largest beetle; the males carry antler-like jaws. Protected.{{< /species >}}
{{< /atlas >}}

## Safety

- **Wild boar:** if you meet a group with young, back away calmly and give them space.
- **Adder:** look where you step and sit on warm, stony slopes; wear closed shoes. After a bite, keep the limb still and call 194.
- **Fire salamander:** harmless if left alone; do not touch your eyes or mouth after handling one.
- **Bison and deer:** watch them only from outside the fences.
