**Spitzer Castle** (Serbian *Špicerov dvorac*, also *Stari dvorac*) stands in Beočin, next to the old cement works, about 7 km from Stari Rakovac. It is a protected cultural monument, but it is in very poor condition and **closed to visitors**.

**Photos:** [the castle outside and inside]({{< relref "gallery/spicer-castle" >}}), photographed by Konstantin Stein in October 2026. **What it looked like originally:** [a reconstruction and 3D model]({{< relref "nearby/spicer-castle-reconstruction" >}}).

## The cement family

Cement made Beočin. Portland cement was produced here from 1867, and in 1887 the works were bought by the company *Redlich, Orenstein und Spitzer* ([021.rs](https://www.021.rs/novi-sad/novosadske-price/311349/spicerov-dvorac-prica-o-neverovatnom-usponu-i-razornoj-moci-nebrige), [Dvorci Srbije](https://www.dvorcisrbije.rs/en/spitzer-castle-beocin/)). Before the First World War the factory employed almost 2,000 workers, and Beočin cement went into the Hungarian Parliament in Budapest ([021.rs](https://www.021.rs/novi-sad/novosadske-price/311349/spicerov-dvorac-prica-o-neverovatnom-usponu-i-razornoj-moci-nebrige)).

The castle was built between **1892 and 1898** as the family villa of **Eduard (Ede) Spitzer**, one of the co-owners ([Beočin municipality](https://www.beocin.rs/sr/?id=1406), [Vojvodina Travel](https://vojvodina.travel/atrakcije/dvorac-spicerovih-beocin/)). He was born in 1845 and married Anna Orenstein, daughter of Baron Heinrich Orenstein ([Dvorci Srbije](https://www.dvorcisrbije.rs/en/spitzer-castle-beocin/)).

## Who designed it

Most sources name **Imre Steindl**, architect of the Hungarian Parliament ([Dvorci Srbije](https://www.dvorcisrbije.rs/en/spitzer-castle-beocin/), [Danas](https://www.danas.rs/vesti/drustvo/spicerov-dvorac-u-beocinu-nekad-velelepna-gradjevina-danas-kuca-duhova/), [Vojvodina Travel](https://vojvodina.travel/atrakcije/dvorac-spicerovih-beocin/)). The municipality also mentions **Ignác Alpár** ([Beočin municipality](https://www.beocin.rs/sr/?id=1406)), and 021.rs writes that Alpár designed Henrik Orenstein's summer residence in Beočin, built of Beočin cement ([021.rs](https://www.021.rs/novi-sad/novosadske-price/311349/spicerov-dvorac-prica-o-neverovatnom-usponu-i-razornoj-moci-nebrige)). The question is still open.

## What it looks like

Romanesque, Gothic, Renaissance and Baroque forms mix with **Hungarian Secession**: an asymmetric two-storey building with a portico, dragons, frogs, snakes and birds in ceramics, plaster and wrought iron, and the turul bird above the entrance. Inside, pink and light blue tones, wood panelling, a gallery around the central hall and a **green Zsolnay ceramic fireplace from Pécs** ([Beočin municipality](https://www.beocin.rs/sr/?id=1406), [Dvorci Srbije](https://www.dvorcisrbije.rs/en/spitzer-castle-beocin/)). Around it lay a walled park with fountains, the *Špicer bašta*; older residents remember deer, roe deer and peacocks there ([Vojvodina Travel](https://vojvodina.travel/atrakcije/dvorac-spicerovih-beocin/)).

## After the Spitzers

The family left for Germany before the Second World War, in 1941 ([Danas](https://www.danas.rs/vesti/drustvo/spicerov-dvorac-u-beocinu-nekad-velelepna-gradjevina-danas-kuca-duhova/)). During the war the castle housed a German military command. After 1945 it was nationalised and served, in turn, as a library, a house of culture, a radio station, a school, a handball club, a home for disabled veterans and finally a restaurant with rooms. The poet **Miroslav Mika Antić** had a painting studio in part of it ([Dvorci Srbije](https://www.dvorcisrbije.rs/en/spitzer-castle-beocin/), [City Magazine](https://citymagazine.danas.rs/lifestyle/retrovizor/nekada-velelepni-vojvodjanski-dvorac-danas-kuca-duhova-bio-je-atelje-mike-antica-tu-je-snimao-i-klint-istvud-a-sada-je-ulaz-zabranjen/)).

After the company that ran it was privatised, the building was abandoned. Part of the entrance porch collapsed in the winter of 2011; the roof has since been repaired so that it does not fall in ([Dvorci Srbije](https://www.dvorcisrbije.rs/en/spitzer-castle-beocin/)). The castle has been a **cultural monument since 18 June 1997** ([Dvorci Srbije](https://www.dvorcisrbije.rs/en/spitzer-castle-beocin/)). Emir Kusturica offered to buy and restore it at his own cost, but the offer was not accepted ([Dvorci Srbije](https://www.dvorcisrbije.rs/en/spitzer-castle-beocin/), [Moj svet u boji](https://www.mojsvetuboji.com/tuzna-prica-o-dvorcu-porodice-spicer/)).

Locals call it the "ghost house". The municipality tells of a parchment found during restoration about a mysterious builder named Nador ([Beočin municipality](https://www.beocin.rs/sr/?id=1406)); we give it as a legend, not as history.

**Where else did Kusturica film?** Sources agree on the Danube bank and Beočin; the Fruškać film map places the Roma settlement of the story on the bank of the Danube, without naming the village ([Fruškać](https://fruskac.net/rs/istorija/kako-tanjug-javlja-filmska-mapa-fruske-gore)). We have not found the exact spot in published sources. If you remember the shooting in the summer of 1997, please write to us.

*The Brothers Bloom* (2008) is sometimes listed among the castle's films ([Dvorci Srbije](https://www.dvorcisrbije.rs/en/spitzer-castle-beocin/)), but its Serbian scenes are documented only in Belgrade, and its castle is Peleș in Romania ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Brothers_Bloom)). We count it as unconfirmed.

## Visiting

The castle is behind a fence and **entry is forbidden**: the building is unstable and people have fallen there ([Dvorci Srbije](https://www.dvorcisrbije.rs/en/spitzer-castle-beocin/), [City Magazine](https://citymagazine.danas.rs/lifestyle/retrovizor/nekada-velelepni-vojvodjanski-dvorac-danas-kuca-duhova-bio-je-atelje-mike-antica-tu-je-snimao-i-klint-istvud-a-sada-je-ulaz-zabranjen/)). You can see it from the street.
