## Raka and the deer

A legend written down in 1704 tells how **Raka Milošević**, chief chamberlain of Despot Jovan Branković, killed a deer at this spot at the end of the 15th century. The deer was a symbol of monastic purity, and Raka vowed to atone by building a church where it fell. Tradition gives the year as 1498, and the monastery and the village took their name from him.

## The first records

History proper begins with the **Ottoman tax registers of 1545–1546**, which list the monastery and the tax it paid. Fragments of frescoes from the first half of the 16th century survive inside the church. During the 17th century the monastery was damaged and abandoned several times and rebuilt each time.

## A monastery of scribes

In the early 18th century Rakovac ran an active scriptorium. Here, in **1700**, the hieromonk Pahomije made a new copy of **Dušan's Code**, the law code of the medieval Serbian state. In **1714** the monk Maksim finished the **Rakovac Srbljak**, a liturgical book collecting the hymns to Serbian ruler-saints, edited by Abbot Teofan.

## The baroque golden age

The 18th century was the monastery's best period. Metropolitan Vićentije Jovanović paid for the tall **baroque bell tower** (1735). Vasa Ostojić painted the icons of the **iconostasis** (1763), Amvrosije Janković painted the refectory (1768), and the residential wings took their final three-sided form in 1771. So many future bishops took their vows or lived here that Rakovac was nicknamed the "court monastery". See [People]({{< relref "people" >}}).

## War and destruction

The monastery was looted in the First World War. In the Second World War, after a **partisan printing press** was discovered inside, the church and bell tower were mined and blown up in 1943, and valuables were taken to Zagreb. The old village around it was burned.

## Rebirth

The church was largely rebuilt in the 1990s, and work on the residential buildings began in 2003 with the help of donations. The monastery was declared a Monument of Culture of Exceptional Importance in 1990. From 1990 to 2020 it was led by Hegumeness Gavrila Bedov. Today it is a men's monastery, with Hieromonk Evgenije (Pilipović) as its abbot ([radiossr.net, 2023](https://radiossr.net/?p=78532)).
