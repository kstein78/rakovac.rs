## Timeline

| When | What happened |
|---|---|
| about 7,000 years ago | Neolithic farmers settle at **Gradina**, in what is now Novi Rakovac. |
| 3rd–4th century AD | A **late Roman villa** with underfloor heating (hypocaust) stands on the same spot. |
| 12th century | A Benedictine abbey, known in Hungarian charters as **Dombó**, builds a three-aisled Romanesque basilica about 32 m long, with a carved marble chancel screen. The exact date of construction is debated. |
| 1237 | The abbot of Dombó ("Abbas de Dombo") appears in a written charter for the first time. |
| 1416 | Abbot George of Dombó is captured by Ottoman raiders. |
| 1467–1473 | The abbey is turned into a fortress with walls, gates and a moat. |
| 1526 | During the Ottoman campaign that ended at Mohács, the fortress of Dombó falls. |
| late 15th century | According to legend, **Raka**, chamberlain of Despot Jovan Branković, founds Rakovac Monastery. |
| 1545–1546 | Rakovac Monastery appears in Ottoman tax registers, its first reliable record. |
| 1700–1714 | The monastery's scriptorium copies **Dušan's Code** (1700) and completes the **Rakovac Srbljak** (1714). |
| 1735–1771 | Baroque rebuilding: bell tower (1735), iconostasis (1763), refectory paintings (1768), residential wings (1771). |
| 1930s | Stone quarrying at **Beli Majdan** comes to an end. |
| 1941–1945 | Srem is occupied. Quarry workers and villagers join the partisans. In 1943 the monastery is looted and blown up and the old village burned. |
| after 1945 | The old village is not rebuilt. **Novi Rakovac** grows further down the valley, and the old part becomes Stari Rakovac. |
| 1990 | The monastery is declared a Monument of Culture of Exceptional Importance. Its church is restored during the 1990s. |
| 2022 | The census counts **2,137** residents. |
