About 500 m from the monastery, beside the road up to Zmajevac, stands the memorial complex known as **Kamenolom** ("the quarry"). It marks the place where in September 1941 two squads swore the partisan oath: one of Rakovac villagers and one of workers from the quarry.

## What you see

By the road, tall concrete pylons, one with a five-pointed star cut through its top, and stone-clad pillars form a gateway. From there a path crosses the lawn to the foot of the hill, where the memorial wall is built into the slope.

{{< photos "kamenolom-path-to-mosaic kamenolom-mosaic-wall" >}}

Above the entrance to the gallery, a mosaic shows **Tito's portrait** with the words *"Druže Tito, mi ti se kunemo"* ("Comrade Tito, we swear to you"). It was painted over in the 1990s; today the portrait and the words are visible again.

Below the portrait runs an abstract mosaic frieze of sharp black, white and ochre shapes, and below that a stone arch that looks like the mouth of a mine gallery, closed with a grille. In front of it lies a stone with a worn inscription. Its legible part says that villagers of Rakovac and workers of the quarry took the partisan oath "on this spot on 24 September 1941".

{{< photos "kamenolom-entrance kamenolom-oath-stone" >}}

A separate stone wall nearby carries four black plaques: the introductory plaque of 1961, the names of the fallen fighters, and two plaques with the names of the victims of fascist terror (see below).

## Behind the grille

The back wall of the gallery carries the text of the partisan oath of 1941, laid in mosaic:

*„Mi, narodni partizani Jugoslavije, latili smo se oružja za nemilosrdnu borbu protiv krvoločnih neprijatelja koji porobiše našu zemlju i istrebljuju naše narode. U ime slobode i pravde našeg naroda zaklinjemo se, da ćemo disciplinovano, uporno i neustrašivo, ne štedeći svoje krvi i živote, voditi borbu do potpunog uništenja fašističkog osvajača i svih narodnih izdajnika.“*

"We, the people's partisans of Yugoslavia, have taken up arms for a merciless struggle against the bloodthirsty enemies who have enslaved our country and are exterminating our peoples. In the name of the freedom and justice of our people we swear that, with discipline, persistence and without fear, sparing neither our blood nor our lives, we will fight until the fascist invader and all traitors of the people are completely destroyed."

Two plaques on the side walls, in newer-looking lettering, name the two squads (*desetine*, "groups of ten"):

**19 September 1941**, the squad of Rakovac villagers: Nikola Mojić · Gligorije Matić Giga · Slavko Mojić · Jova Belić · Draga Kadić · Đoka Mojić · Božo Jović · Đurica Mojić · Branko Matić · Branko Božanić-Brinja.

**24 September 1941**, the workers' squad of the Rakovac quarry: Toma Banjanin · Boža Čanković · Dušan Savić · Dušan Nedimović · Dragan Skular · Rista Vukelja · Anđelko Jureškov · Đura Čujzek · Mita Belić.

## The plaque of 1961

The introductory plaque reads, in Serbian: *„Rakovac selo i Kamenolom iako su mali i u blizini jakih fašističkih uporišta nisu se predali okupatoru. Pod rukovodstvom Komunističke partije odvažnih radnih ljudi iz mesta i Kamenoloma ubrzo su počeli zadavati udarce okupatoru. Borilo se i ginulo. Već početkom 1942 god. uništena je okupatorska vlast. Rakovac i Kamenolom se ponose svojim žrtvama i učešćem u oslobodilačkoj borbi naših naroda. 1941–1961.“*

In English: "Although small and close to strong fascist strongholds, the village of Rakovac and Kamenolom did not surrender to the occupier. Led by the Communist Party, brave working people from the village and the quarry soon began to strike at the occupier. They fought and they died. By the beginning of 1942 the occupiers' authority had been destroyed. Rakovac and Kamenolom are proud of their victims and of their part in the liberation struggle of our peoples. 1941–1961." The date on this plaque is the basis for dating the memorial to 1961.

## Names on the memorial

Transcribed from our photographs of October 2026, in the order and spelling of the plaques (surname first). In the plaque lettering the letters Л (L) and П (P) look almost the same, and a few letters are worn, so single names may be misread. Corrections are welcome. Some names appear more than once: Mita Belić, Dušan Savić and Rista Vukelja are listed both in the workers' squad and among the fallen fighters, and Slavko Mojić both in the villagers' squad and among the fighters.
